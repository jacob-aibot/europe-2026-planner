## 1. What all three share, and why that is not convergence

Emil's `prototype` skill, hard rule 2: *"Sharing the project's tokens is not convergence. Variants
should feel native to the product."* So the three agree on everything that is **product truth** and
differ on everything that is **design**.

**Shared — the same data, enforced rather than promised.** `directions/model.js` derives the record
once and all three import it. Jacob's requirement was *"the same representative Cairn content so I
can compare design rather than data differences"*; a shared module is how that is made true instead
of asserted.

**Shared — the palette, sampled not invented.** Every hue in `directions/tokens.css` was measured off
the board PNG by `sample-board.mjs`. The accent `#d4503a` is Jacob's own — the "CAIRN" eyebrow and
the "PRIMARY" pill, measured independently in two regions and identical in both.

**Shared — the map rule**, which is the direct answer to *"disconnected geographic fragments without
a clear world or journey context"*:

1. **All land is always drawn**, continuously, in one tone. The world is never a set of fragments.
2. **Visited is an overlay laid on that land**, never a replacement for it — Polarsteps' translucent
   wash over terrain, translated to a product that has no imagery.
3. **The route is white.** Solid where travelled, dashed where not yet (AllTrails' own convention).
4. **The accent is spent on one thing only**: what is selected, or what is happening now.
5. **Tonal contrast between map and data is always strong** — white sheet on a dark map, or a dark
   card on a light map. Never grey on grey.

**Different — composition, interaction model, and tonal world.** That is the axis table in §3.

## 2. The data, and exactly how truthful it is

| Source | What it is | Honest status |
|---|---|---|
| `apps/web/src/sample/europe2026.json` | The **real** Europe 2026 trip: 16 days, 112 stops (all 112 carry a coordinate), 95 places, 21 bookings, 6 cities, real titles, real times, real prices, real ticket flags | Real. Generated by `tools/gen-sample.mjs`, redacted per ARCHITECTURE §6.6 — no door PINs, booking references or ticket URLs |
| `qa/i8b-render.mjs`'s reference library | Central Europe 2019, Croatia 2022, London 2026, Japan 2027 | **Development fixture, NOT Jacob's actual travel history.** Carried across unchanged so the directions can show completed-vs-planned and country-level records without anyone inventing a trip. Stated here rather than left to be discovered |
| `packages/core/src/geo/countries.gen.ts` | 239 ISO codes of Natural Earth admin-0 at 1:110m | Real, public domain, already committed and already what `countryOf` attributes against |
| `directions/data/region50.json` | The same Natural Earth release at 1:50m, Europe window + a few outliers | Real. sha256 verified byte-identical to the hash `countries.gen.ts` already records. **Drawn at a finer scale than attribution runs at** — standard cartographic practice, and it changes no attribution, no framing and no `SUMMARY_VERSION`. `build-region.mjs` explains why in full |

**Behaviour deliberately omitted because Cairn does not have the data.** Every one of these was a
place a prettier mock could have been made, and was not:

- **Photographs.** No `PhotoAsset`, no library, nothing until Phase 6. Direction B reserves genuinely
  large photographic surfaces and renders them **empty and labelled**. The audit asserts zero `<img>`
  and zero background images across all three directions, so this is checked, not promised.
- **Achievements, streaks, badges, goals, "16% of the world".** Polarsteps' travel-stats card is on
  the board; Cairn has no such model and none is drawn.
- **Friends, avatars, participants, "now travelling".** Polarsteps' second globe is full of other
  people's faces. Cairn has no social graph yet, so its globe has none.
- **Live position, route traces, distance by mode, elevation.** AllTrails' elevation profile is on
  the board and has no Cairn equivalent. The one distance shown (`3,466 km between cities`) is a
  great-circle sum computed from real coordinates and is labelled as what it is.
- **City coordinates for past-trip records.** `TripSummaryRow` genuinely has none (A-40 Part 5), so
  those records are drawn at **country scale and never tighter** — `model.js`'s `minSpan` floor
  exists to stop the frame implying precision the record does not have.

## 3. The three directions

### Direction A — Immersive journey map

*`a-journey-map/` · dark · the map is the page*

- **Composition.** There is no page. A dark map fills the viewport at every width and every state; a
  single **white** sheet rides on it. That tonal split is Polarsteps', read off the board, and it is
  the exact inverse of the grey-on-grey the rejected direction shipped.
- **Interaction model.** One gesture: **drill in**. World → journey → day. Each step re-frames the
  map and re-fills the one sheet. Back is the same gesture reversed. `Esc` also goes back.
- **Desktop recomposition.** Not the phone stretched. The sheet becomes a permanent 400px left rail
  **and** a years ribbon (2019–2027) appears along the bottom: three things visible at once where the
  phone shows one.
- **Signature.** The globe — real orthographic projection over the repo's own polygons, a cyan
  atmospheric limb, a sparse deterministic starfield, and the actual LAX→Vienna great circle drawn as
  the shape it really is.
- **What it borrows from the board.** Polarsteps almost entirely: the immersive dark globe, the
  atmosphere, the white sheet with a grab handle, the white route, the single chromatic mark.
- **What makes it recognisably Cairn.** It admits its holes on the primary surface: the unattributed
  count sits in the sheet under the journey list, and the home base is labelled *"Los Angeles ·
  home"* precisely because the record stores it as a coordinate with no country, so the United States
  is deliberately **not** lit. No competitor's globe explains why a country is dark.
- **Strongest quality.** It reads as one coherent world in the first half-second, which is the exact
  thing the rejected map failed at.
- **Principal risk.** At world scale Cairn's real record is small — five countries, four of them
  adjacent. The globe is beautiful and nearly empty, and it will stay that way until the record
  grows. A new user's globe is emptier still.
- **Where it deliberately differs from the references.** Polarsteps' ground is satellite imagery;
  Cairn's cannot be (offline, no tile service, settled). So the *structure* transfers and the texture
  does not, and the land is a single flat tone by necessity rather than by taste.

### Direction B — Photography and cartography

*`b-plates/` · light editorial ground, dark plates · the story is the page*

- **Composition.** A light editorial page on the board's own `#f2efe9`, paced by full-bleed
  **plates** — one place, rendered as cartography at photographic scale, with the day's real route
  and stops across it. A dark data card floats on the plate (AllTrails/Strava's own move).
- **Interaction model.** A vertical **narrative scroll**. Not drill-in, not pan-zoom. The story runs
  top to bottom and the plates arrive as you go.
- **Desktop recomposition.** The page splits: the plate becomes **sticky** for the height of its
  section while the day-by-day and its stops scroll beside it. The relationship between map and text
  becomes visible instead of remembered.
- **Signature.** The plate. It is the emotional surface Cairn can honestly make today.
- **What it borrows from the board.** The Condé Nast card (let one image dominate at genuinely large
  scale), AllTrails/Strava (route as artifact; dark readout on a light map), Apple Journal (the
  memory cluster), Airbnb (generous spacing, premium hierarchy, calm chrome).
- **What makes it recognisably Cairn.** Apple Journal's own cluster puts a **map tile in the grid
  beside its photographs** — so Cairn's cluster does too, and the tiles it can fill are maps while
  the ones it cannot are left visibly empty and labelled *"Photo · none yet"*, with a note saying no
  library is connected. That is a design that tells the truth about its own age.
- **Strongest quality.** It is the only one of the three that makes a completed trip feel like
  something worth re-reading rather than something worth querying.
- **Principal risk — and it is real.** Impeccable's detector independently flagged B's plates as
  `shape-assembled-illustration`: *"an inline SVG scene of primitive shapes standing in for an
  illustration."* Judged a false positive (they are real Natural Earth polygons, not decorative
  shapes) — but the rule is pointing at B's actual thesis, which is **cartography standing in for
  photography**. If Jacob's reaction to a plate is *"that is a map pretending to be a picture"*, that
  is the direction failing, not the detector misfiring.
- **Where it deliberately differs from the references.** Every reference on the board fills these
  surfaces with real photographs. B cannot, and does not pretend to.

### Direction C — Spatial exploration

*`c-spatial/` · light, near-achromatic · the canvas is the page*

- **Composition.** One continuous canvas in Cosmos's own register — measured, not guessed: the most
  saturated pixel anywhere in Cosmos's hero is `#e2e1de`, median luma 245. Confidence comes from
  scale, space and weight. Controlled asymmetry: labels sit off-axis against the geography they
  belong to, at deliberately unequal sizes.
- **Interaction model.** **Semantic zoom**, three levels, where moving between them changes *what
  things are*, not how big they are. World: a journey is one mark and its disc **is** its duration.
  Countries: countries resolve, each with its own years. Places: the real trip's cities and stops
  resolve. Drag to pan at every level; `+`/`−` and arrow keys change level.
- **Desktop recomposition.** The canvas keeps its full behaviour **and** a permanent 320px index
  opens beside it — everything the record holds, always legible, highlighting with the canvas. Two
  ways of holding the same space at once.
- **Signature.** Duration as area, and a label that is allowed to move away from its geography with a
  leader line holding the two together.
- **What it borrows from the board.** Cosmos: visual rhythm, unequal scale, discovery, restraint that
  is not emptiness.
- **What makes it recognisably Cairn.** The "holes" note is pinned into the composition as a
  first-class element rather than a footnote, and country-level records visibly **never resolve past
  Countries** — the zoom itself tells you how precise the data is.
- **Strongest quality.** It is the only one where the shape of the record — five journeys, one big,
  one not yet taken — is legible at a glance without reading a single row.
- **Principal risk.** Four of five journeys sit inside Europe, so at world scale their labels must be
  de-collided downward, and on a phone that produces a stack with long leader lines that edges toward
  looking like a list with decoration. It is the direction most likely to feel clever rather than
  useful.
- **Where it deliberately differs from the references.** Cosmos is an image-bookmarking product and
  Jacob explicitly ruled Cairn must not become one. C takes Cosmos's *spatial confidence* and applies
  it to geography rather than to a grid of images.

### The axes, so the divergence is checkable

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | dark, immersive | light editorial + dark plates | light, near-achromatic |
| The page is | a map | a story | a canvas |
| Navigation | drill in / back | scroll | zoom + pan |
| Desktop adds | a rail **and** a time ribbon | a sticky plate beside scrolling text | a permanent index beside the canvas |
| Primary artifact | the globe | the plate | the spatial layout |
| Motion | **none** | **none** | one transition (zoom) |

## 4. Critique — what the tools actually found

### 4.1 Impeccable detector, rendered, both viewports

`node cairn/.claude/skills/impeccable/scripts/detect.mjs --viewport <WxH> <url>`

| Direction | 1440×900 | 390×844 |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **clean** | **clean** |
| **B** | 5 | 5 |
| **C** | 3 | 2 |

**Everything it found, and the judgement on each:**

| Finding | Where | Judgement |
|---|---|---|
| `script-error: proj is not defined` | A | **REAL, and the most valuable finding of the pass.** A refactor had moved the country hit-disc loop inside a helper, so every map label threw and every country's 44px tap target was missing — on screenshots I had already looked at and approved. Fixed. This is the case for running the detector rather than trusting the eye |
| `undersized-ui-text` 10.3125px ×9 | A, C | **REAL.** `font-size` was set on `html` as well as `body`, which redefines what `rem` means; every `rem` token was silently scaled by 0.9375 and the 11px micro label rendered at 10.3125px. Invisible by eye, obvious to the rule. Fixed |
| `low-contrast` 1.2:1 ×20 | B | **REAL.** The dark readout card was translucent over the light visited wash; worst-case pixel contrast fell to 1.2:1. Made solid, blur removed |
| `low-contrast` 3.7:1 — `#d4503a` on `#f2efe9` | B, C | **REAL, and it forced a token.** Jacob's board accent fails AA as *text*. Split into `--accent` (marks and fills, which answer to 3:1) and `--accent-ink: #b8412c` (measured 4.78:1) for anything that is a word someone must read |
| `low-contrast` 2.8:1 — gold on cream | B | **REAL.** Replaced with the shipped product's own measured `#8f5816` (QA R34-7) |
| `low-contrast` 1.1:1 — ink on space | A | **REAL ×2.** The year ribbon inherited body ink onto the dark map. Also: an SVG `<text>` that sets only `fill` still computes `color` from inherited ink, so the map labels read as dark-on-dark to any checker. Both fixed |
| `kicker-above-heading` ×4 | B | **REAL, and it had to go.** Impeccable bans the eyebrow outright — *"no brief earns it back"* — and it is one of the three tells cited as mechanical evidence against the rejected direction. Shipping it in the replacement would have been indefensible. All four deleted; the place name became a byline **under** the heading |
| `shape-assembled-illustration` ×3 | B | **Judged false positive, and reported anyway.** These are real Natural Earth polygons. But see B's principal risk above: the rule is pointing at something true about the direction |
| `cream-palette` | A, B, C | **Judged, not suppressed.** The rule is right in general and Anthropic's `frontend-design` skill names the same cluster. But `#f2efe9` was *sampled from Jacob's own board*, and Impeccable's own SKILL.md says *"The brief wins… Redirecting a clear brief toward your taste is failure."* Not changed, not silently ignored, and no ignore-rule was written — it is left visible so Jacob decides |
| `tiny-text` 11px | B, C | **Accepted by contract.** 11px is the product's settled `--ui-text-floor` |
| `em-dash-overuse` (advisory) | B | Partly acted on: the visible prototype copy was reworded, 18 → 16 |

### 4.2 The rendered audit — Jacob's own verification list

`node cairn/docs/design/directions/audit.mjs` → **ALL CHECKS PASS**, 3 directions × 2 viewports.

Asserted, not asserted-about: no page or console errors · no horizontal overflow · nothing past the
right edge · map occupies ≥25% of the first viewport · all land drawn, not only the visited part ·
visited marked as an overlay · every pointer target ≥24×24 (WCAG 2.5.8) · every HTML button ≥44px
tall on a phone · every focusable element shows a ≥2px focus ring · no control exists only on
`:hover` · every transition resolves to 0s under `prefers-reduced-motion` · no animation over 300ms ·
`viewport-fit=cover` paired with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` · no rendered text below 11px · **zero
photographic assets**.

Two probe defects were found and fixed rather than worked around: it counted favicon 404s as page
errors, and it counted SVG children (clipped by their viewBox) as page overflow.

Three real defects it caught that the detector did not:

- **A's country shapes were a second, smaller accessible target** beside their 44px hit discs — 8–25px
  at world scale. The shape stays tappable; the semantics moved to the disc.
- **The custom typefaces never loaded.** `tokens.css` referenced `../data/…` but sits *in*
  `directions/`, so every render up to that point was in a fallback system face. Jacob would have
  been judging the wrong typeface. Fixed; re-rendered.
- **A country-level record was framed to a ~50km box** around its key point — a picture claiming far
  more precision than the record has. `model.js` now imposes a country-sized floor.

### 4.3 Emil `review-animations` — the ten standards

Findings, and the remedial step taken:

- **A: dead transition deleted** (hierarchy step 1). `#frame` transitioned opacity and nothing ever
  changed its opacity. More importantly, an animated map re-frame between two different projections
  is not a tween — the shapes are not the same shapes — so a fade over a re-render is decoration
  pretending to be continuity. The shipped product fences it for an independent reason (A-40 W1: the
  frame is measurement-free by ruling). **Direction A now has no animation at all.**
- **B: no animation, deliberately.** Scrolling is in standard 2's 100+/session bucket, where the
  correct amount of motion is zero. `scroll-behavior: smooth` affects programmatic jumps only and is
  off under reduced motion.
- **C: one animation, and it earns its place.** The semantic-zoom transition — 260ms (standard 4),
  `cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)` decelerating and never `ease-in` (standard 3), `transform` only and
  GPU (standard 7), a CSS transition so a second press retargets from wherever the first got to
  (standard 6), suppressed entirely during a drag because direct manipulation must track the finger
  with no easing, and zeroed under `prefers-reduced-motion` (standard 8).
- **All three: `:hover` gated** behind `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` (standard 8) — on
  touch a tapped hover state otherwise sticks until the next tap elsewhere.
- No `transition: all`, no `scale(0)`, no keyframes, no layout-property animation, no entrance
  motion, no stagger, no scroll-reveal anywhere.

*Not done: Jacob's instruction was "do not add motion merely to make the mockup appear
sophisticated." Two of three directions therefore have none, and that is the finding, not an
omission.*
